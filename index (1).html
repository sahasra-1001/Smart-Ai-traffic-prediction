Route advisor

Counting vehicles is only the input. What a driver needs is how long the trip will take and whether another road is faster. This uses the time you pick on the forecast slider above.

Travel time = route length ÷ expected speed. Expected speed falls as the predicted traffic volume approaches the road’s capacity. Routes B and C carry a share of the monitored road’s volume. These are illustrative values, not measured data.

How the system works

Nine stages take raw road video to a congestion estimate.

  1. Roadside video inputCCTV / road
  2. Frame preprocessingOpenCV: 1 frame/second, resize, normalize
  3. Vehicle detectionYOLOv8: class, confidence, box
  4. Vehicle trackingByteTrack assigns IDs
  5. Vehicle countingCar, bus, truck, bike
  6. Traffic data generationCounts every 5 min, CSV
  7. Short-term forecastingLSTM predicts next volume
  8. Congestion estimationLow, medium or high
  9. VisualizationGraphs and analysis

Project details

Problem

Traffic monitoring still relies on manual observation or static sensors, which lack real-time analysis and the ability to predict what happens next.

Objectives

  • Detect and classify vehicles from roadside video
  • Count and track vehicles accurately
  • Estimate traffic flow, volume and density
  • Predict short-term traffic conditions
  • Build a prototype for real-time analysis

Dataset

  • Roadside CCTV / traffic video, 1920 × 1080
  • Car, bus, truck and bike classes
  • Frames extracted at fixed intervals
  • Annotated in YOLO format

Technologies

PythonOpenCVYOLOv8TensorFlow (LSTM)Flask (prototype)HTML / CSS / JSPandasMatplotlibNumPyVS Code

Key outcomes

  • Accurate vehicle detection and classification with YOLOv8
  • Reliable counting and traffic volume estimation
  • Effective short-term forecasting with an LSTM
  • Estimates of traffic density and congestion levels

Limitations

  • Accuracy drops in rain, fog or low light
  • Occlusion and heavy traffic affect tracking
  • Data limited to specific locations and vehicle types
  • Speed estimation is approximate
  • Real-time use needs better hardware

Future direction

  • More diverse datasets and road conditions
  • DeepSORT or StrongSORT for tracking
  • Speed and lane-wise analysis
  • GRU or Transformer for longer horizons
  • Incident detection and adaptive signal control

Conclusion

Combining deep learning detection with time-series forecasting gives a working approach to real-time traffic monitoring and short-term congestion prediction, which can support proactive traffic management and road safety in smart cities.
